# Factor information research (load only for this domain)

Question: for specified objects, observation time, outcome horizon and existing information B, what does added information X change about outcome Y? Information usefulness and complete trading-strategy profitability are different questions; failure for one use does not disprove all other uses, nor authorize searching those other uses for a winner.

## From intuition to an executable study

“研究透” means the necessary checks for the stated use and available evidence are resolved, with the remaining boundary explicit. It does not mean all markets, horizons and parameter choices have been tried. Use one primary decision question; direction, return and risk are separate uses. Report unstudied uses explicitly instead of silently expanding the study.

1. **Preserve the intuition.** Record the user's original claim, its proposed mechanism as a hypothesis, the observable candidate X, the outcome Y and information already available B. State what result would weaken the claim. Resolve scope and horizon from existing context first. A reasonable research-only default may be chosen before viewing results, labeled provisional; do not guess an established business definition or change the user's meaning. When alternative interpretations would reverse the question and no authority resolves them, block only that branch.
2. **Produce a definition and working computation.** Reuse the project's exact definition ID/version. For a new candidate, write a local versioned definition with formula, parameters, unit/direction, inputs and adjustments, universe, frequency, missing/warmup treatment, dependencies, observation time, availability time and earliest permissible decision. Reference the authoritative definition rather than copying it into a competing registry. Candidate registration or computability does not prove effectiveness. Supply actual code or a verified existing implementation and calculated values, not just pseudocode.
3. **Check meaning before measuring performance.** Independently calculate a few examples including a boundary, missing values and time alignment. Compare to the implementation. Audit price/label start, costs if relevant, future leakage, eligible and excluded counts, instrument history and source/code fingerprints. Record defects and corrections; do not silently repair an old frozen result. Future outcome columns never enter a signal or fitted input.
4. **Freeze a compact design before execution.** Select a primary outcome/horizon and relevant simple baseline, common eligible population, weights, effect units and dependence-aware uncertainty method. Specify essential checks, their costs and stopping conditions in the existing contract/ledger. Preplan at least one meaningful period/object/concentration check where data permit. Design can adapt to demonstrated evidence gaps; each new branch must be registered before its run and marked exploratory if chosen after inspecting results. A finite budget is not permission to disguise a grid as one experiment.
5. **Measure performance and added usefulness.** First measure the factor's actual behavior against the reference. Then test what X changes after B is already known; raw group differences or correlation with B are not incremental usefulness. Use baseline-only, factor-only and combined comparisons when a prediction model is appropriate, with time-respecting fitting/evaluation. For state descriptions, use compatible conditional groups/standardized comparisons, show support and sparse or incomparable cells, and label the result descriptive. Do not force a fitted model on a question that does not need it.
6. **Try to overturn the interpretation.** Check whether a few dates, episodes or instruments drive it; relevant periods/groups have contradictory effects; overlap makes apparent sample size misleading; a simpler known signal explains it. Choose the smallest checks that can change the conclusion within scope. Don't mechanically add every statistical method, try parameters until positive, or invent return/cost metrics for a non-trading factor. Unseen evidence is a separate status: reused history, shuffled splits and later-selected periods do not become independent confirmation.
7. **Reconcile and finish.** Independently recalculate at least the headline baseline/candidate/difference and usable counts from saved outputs. Reconcile overall and subgroup results. Save definition/code, source manifest, trial ledger (all versions/failures), results, report and resume state using the project's artifacts. Deliver the final factor table, increment table and plain conclusion together; no intermediate research results by default.

## Completion review

Before finalizing, save a short coverage table in the report appendix or existing manifest. Each row needs `complete`, `not_applicable`, `evidence_boundary`, `pending` or `blocked`, artifact references and a concrete reason. Cover: definition; implementation and numerical checks; data/time quality; performance; baseline increment; uncertainty/dependence; stability/counterexamples; independence/selection history; reproducibility and final reconciliation.

`complete` means a check ran with evidence, not merely that its title exists. `not_applicable` needs a question-specific reason. Limited modes retain their boundary and label unrun checks accordingly. For full research, any core `pending` or `blocked` item prevents claiming the factor study complete. Missing a computable definition, an available fair baseline or necessary uncertainty analysis cannot be renamed an evidence boundary. Necessary affordable checks run now; budget exhaustion pauses with pending items. When current-history checks are complete but genuinely new evidence is unavailable, close only the historical scope and say independent confirmation is absent. The fallback checker validates lifecycle/artifact consistency, **not this scientific coverage review**; the controller must inspect the actual evidence.

Give a use-specific decision: retain as a candidate, no improvement found over the stated baseline, current data cannot distinguish useful improvement from chance, or cannot evaluate yet. Keep definition correctness, predictive usefulness and trading profitability separate. A strategy performance claim requires its own authorized execution rules, cost/turnover and full capital accounting; factor research alone does not establish it.

Choose methods for the question:

| Question | Appropriate evidence |
|---|---|
| Same-day stock ranking | Date-level rank correlation (how closely factor ranking tracks later outcome ranking), small groups, relevant exposures if justified. |
| Single ETF/index state | Fixed-horizon conditional returns, event frequencies or risk, with a simple relevant baseline; no forced cross-sectional correlation. |
| Probability/risk | Fix event, horizon, threshold; baseline event frequency, coverage and event count; prediction error/calibration only if an actual probability predictor exists. Historical group frequency alone is not a probability model. |
| Portfolio attribution | Matching market, currency, frequency and return conventions; realized later factor returns cannot be inputs known earlier. |
| Engineering/literature | Definition/implementation checks or source-backed claim/counterclaim comparisons, not mandatory financial metrics. |

For dual moving averages, inspect exact state definitions first. Compare single SMA, single EMA, same-horizon prior return and historical volatility when relevant, rather than importing a famous factor model by name. “After SMA indicates this state, does EMA confirmation change future outcomes?” and the converse are minimal questions. Use a common eligible date universe, but acknowledge the groups contain different actual dates. Match or stratify only using information known at observation time when necessary. If B already contains complete SMA/EMA states, their deterministic combination adds no raw information; test representation/interaction usefulness for the specified model instead.

Before statistics, check signal known time, entry/label starting time, horizon/end date, missing labels, weights, denominators, cross-boundary exclusions, survivorship and data fingerprints. Loss from initial price to future minimum differs from the largest future peak-to-trough decline. Do not include pre-signal losses in later-outcome labels.

Overlapping returns, continuous states and related ETFs share information. Report dates, consecutive-state events and actual usable coverage; neither dates nor event counts automatically mean independent opportunities. Uncertainty estimates must retain relevant date/cross-asset dependence and admit limits. Arithmetic and definition checks come first; add robustness only when it can change the question's judgment.

Label evidence separately:

- A: raw difference against a simple reference; can contain confounding.
- B: conditional increment over an explicit baseline; scope limited to that baseline.
- C: same question validated on qualified independent new evidence, not reused history, reshuffling or repeated calculations.

Do not automatically promote A to B or B to C. Classical published results, local recalculations and engineering fixtures are distinct provenance. A wide uncertainty range containing improvement and no improvement means evidence cannot distinguish them; not proof of ineffectiveness. Preserve exploratory branches and known family attempt history; unknown history stays unknown. Do not invent a meaningful-effect threshold after observing results.
